# Protocol: Hip and Proximal Femur Trauma (PROT-HIP-01)

## Scope
Acute hip, groin, or proximal femur injuries from contact, falls, or high-energy trauma in professional football. Includes suspected fracture, severe contusion, and post-fixation rehab coordination with orthopaedics.

## Red Flags (urgent)
- Inability to weight-bear
- Severe pain (typically ≥8/10) after tackle/fall onto hip
- Shortened or externally rotated limb
- Neurovascular concern
- X-ray showing displaced proximal femur / hip fracture

## Acute Management
1. Stop play/training; stretcher if non-weight-bearing.
2. Analgesia; immobilise; urgent pelvis/hip X-ray.
3. If displaced fracture: ED / trauma ortho pathway same day.
4. If X-ray negative/indeterminate but suspicion high: non-weight-bearing + MRI within 24–48 h.

## RTP Framework (guidance only — individualise with ortho)
| Stage | Criteria focus |
|-------|----------------|
| 1 | Pain control, wound/healing, protected ROM |
| 2 | Progressive weight-bearing per imaging/surgeon |
| 3 | Strength, hydrotherapy, bike/alter-G |
| 4 | Field reconditioning, non-contact football |
| 5 | Full training + match minutes after clearance |

Displaced surgically fixed proximal femur injuries commonly require **months**, not weeks. Contusion / bone bruise without complete fracture may return in **4–8 weeks** if MRI clears and strength/pain criteria are met.

## Club Notes
- Do not invent fixed RTP dates without imaging + clinical clearance.
- Coordinate GPS load ramp with medical after return to pitch.
- Severity for triage: treat clear displaced hip/proximal femur fracture as **severity 5**.
