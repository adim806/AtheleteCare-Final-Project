# Case Report: Displaced Proximal Femur / Hip Fracture – Defender

## Incident Summary
Defender sustained a heavy tackle and fall onto the right hip during a competitive match. Immediate inability to weight-bear. Severe pain (9/10). Removed by stretcher. No prior hip complaints.

## Initial Diagnosis
- **Injury**: Displaced proximal femur fracture (intertrochanteric / subtrochanteric region).
- **Mechanism**: High-energy contact, lateral fall onto hip after tackle.
- **Imaging**: Pelvis/hip X-ray showed clear displaced fracture; CT used for surgical planning. No pelvic ring disruption.

## Treatment Timeline
| Phase | Intervention |
|-------|-------------|
| Day 0 | ED transfer, analgesia, traction, urgent ortho review |
| Day 1–2 | ORIF / surgical fixation per trauma ortho plan |
| Week 1–2 | Non-weight-bearing, DVT prophylaxis, wound care |
| Week 3–6 | Protected weight-bearing progression as allowed by surgeon |
| Week 7–12 | Hydrotherapy, hip ROM, progressive strengthening |
| Month 4–5 | Field reconditioning, bike/alter-G, non-contact football drills |
| Month 6 | Gradual return to full training contingent on imaging + strength symmetry |

## Outcome
Player returned to full team training at approximately **180 days**. First competitive minutes after medical clearance and GPS ramp. No hardware complication. Ongoing monitoring for stiffness and load tolerance.

## Clinical Insight
Acute displaced proximal femur fracture in football is time-critical: early imaging, surgical fixation, and staged RTP under ortho + club medical co-management. Severity should be treated as maximal (imaging abnormality score 5 equivalent). Do not accelerate sprint/contact until radiographic healing and strength criteria are met.
