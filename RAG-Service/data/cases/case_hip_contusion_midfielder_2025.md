# Case Report: Severe Hip Contusion with Occult Fracture Work-up – Midfielder

## Incident Summary
Central midfielder landed awkwardly on the left hip after an aerial duel in training. Immediate groin/hip pain (8/10), antalgic gait, unable to continue. Pain reproduced with log-roll and axial load. No audible pop.

## Initial Diagnosis
- **Injury**: Severe hip contusion; occult proximal femur / femoral neck fracture ruled out on advanced imaging after suspicious plain films.
- **Mechanism**: Direct impact + twisting fall onto hip.
- **Imaging**: Initial pelvis/hip X-ray indeterminate; MRI confirmed bone bruise of proximal femur without complete cortical break. Weight-bearing held until MRI clearance.

## Treatment Timeline
| Week | Intervention |
|------|-------------|
| 0–1 | Crutches, relative rest, analgesia, serial clinical review |
| 1–2 | MRI confirmation; start protected ROM and isometrics |
| 2–3 | Progressive weight-bearing, pool running |
| 3–4 | Bike, strength, jog progression if pain-free |
| 4–5 | Non-contact pitch work, change-of-direction drills |
| 6 | Full training return after medical clearance |

## Outcome
Player returned to full training at **42 days**. No delayed fracture identified. GPS high-speed running capped for first two weeks back per load policy.

## Clinical Insight
Hip pain after contact falls can mask occult proximal femur injury. When X-ray is unclear or clinical suspicion is high, withhold aggressive loading until MRI. Club precedent: treat as high-severity hip trauma until imaging excludes fracture, then step down RTP accordingly.
